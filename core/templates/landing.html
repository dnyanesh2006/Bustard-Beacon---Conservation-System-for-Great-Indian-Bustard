About BUSTARD BEACON

Safeguarding India's Most Threatened Grassland Flagship

BUSTARD BEACON is a specialized wildlife conservation and monitoring platform designed to help authorized teams organize field information and support coordinated protection of bustards and their habitats.

With fewer than 150 Great Indian Bustards remaining in the wild, fragmented grasslands in Solapur, Maharashtra, and the Thar desert represent critical refuges. By linking field rangers on patrol with forest officers, veterinary intervention units, and scientific researchers, BUSTARD BEACON turns every ground observation into an immediate, coordinated conservation response.

Species Portfolio

The Bustards of the Indian Subcontinent

Four distinct bustard species protected under coordinated conservation initiatives across grasslands, semi-arid savannahs, and alluvial plains.

Critically Endangered

Great Indian Bustard

Ardeotis nigriceps

A flagship grassland species and one of the heaviest flying birds in the world. Easily recognized by its large size, horizontal body, long white neck, and distinct black crown. Endemic to the Indian subcontinent, it is now critically endangered.

  • Family: Otididae
  • Body Weight: Up to 15 kg (males)
  • Habitat: Semi-arid short grasslands & agro-pastoral scrub
  • Key Threat: Overhead power-line collision & habitat loss
Endangered

Lesser Florican

Sypheotides indicus

The smallest member of the bustard family. Males feature an entirely black head and neck with distinctive ribbon-like ear feathers, famous for spectacular vertical leap courtship displays during the monsoon breeding season.

  • Family: Otididae
  • Courtship: Up to 500 vertical jumps per day
  • Habitat: Mosaic grasslands & agricultural fallows
  • Distribution: Western & central India during monsoon
Critically Endangered

Bengal Florican

Houbaropsis bengalensis

A rare and elusive bustard heavily adapted to tall alluvial grasslands. The male displays contrasting jet-black plumage with bright white wings. Habitat fragmentation and grassland conversion remain severe existential threats.

  • Family: Otididae
  • Habitat: Lowland alluvial floodplains (Terai & Brahmaputra)
  • Status: Fewer than 1,000 mature individuals worldwide
  • Protection: Strict sanctuary riverine zone monitoring
Vulnerable

Macqueen's Bustard

Chlamydotis macqueenii

Also known as the Asian Houbara. Unlike resident species, it is a winter migrant to India, settling in hyper-arid deserts and salt flats of Rajasthan and Gujarat with cryptic sandy-buff plumage engineered for desert camouflage.

  • Family: Otididae
  • Migration: Winters from Central Asia into northwest India
  • Habitat: Arid gravel plains, dunes, and salt flats
  • Adaptation: Highly cryptic sand-toned plumage
