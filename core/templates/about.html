Official Context

Great Indian Bustard (*Ardeotis nigriceps*)

Conservation Status

Critically Endangered under IUCN Red List & Schedule I of Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

Sanctuary Headquarters

Great Indian Bustard Sanctuary, Nannaj / Solapur District, Maharashtra, India.

1. Species & Habitat Overview

The Great Indian Bustard is an iconic grassland bird species characterized by its large body size, horizontal body carriage, long bare legs, and distinctive black crown on the forehead. Adult males weigh up to 15 kg and display elaborate courtship displays in short-grass prairies during breeding seasons.

2. Nannaj / Solapur Geography

Solapur district in Maharashtra encompasses key semi-arid grassland ecosystems. Official Maharashtra forest administration recognizes Nannaj as a premier sanctuary headquarters. The sanctuary features core breeding pockets, seasonal buffer migration corridors, and regulated eco-tourism zones.

3. Primary Conservation Threats
  • Power Line Collisions: Poor frontal vision makes bustards vulnerable to high-tension power cables. Installation of bird flight diverters is a priority intervention.
  • Habitat Loss & Encroachment: Conversion of natural short grasslands into intensive agriculture or industrial plots.
  • Nest Disturbance & Predation: Ground-nesting habits expose single-egg clutches to feral dogs and disturbance during incubation.
4. Data Provenance & Location Protection Policy

To prevent poaching threats and unmonitored human intrusion into sensitive nesting areas, exact operational coordinates are strictly restricted based on user authorization level (Ranger, Forest Officer, Medical Team). Public research views feature generalized grid locations.
